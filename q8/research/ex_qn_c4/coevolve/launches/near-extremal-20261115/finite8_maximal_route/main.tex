\title{A target-preserving maximal-counterexample reduction in $Q_8$}
\maketitle
\begin{proposition}
Conditional on the recorded historical arbitrary-degree $Q_6$ weighted
inequality, any counterexample to $\operatorname{ex}(Q_8,C_4)\le682$
has an inclusion-maximal counterexample extension $H$ whose edge count
$m$ is in $\{683,684,685,686,687,688\}$. For either parity, its degree
histogram $n=(n_0,\ldots,n_8)$ satisfies
\[
n_0=0,\quad n_d\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad\sum n_d=128,
\quad\sum dn_d=m,
\]
\[
\sum C_dn_d\le138432-201m,\qquad
\sum b_dn_d\le6912-10m,
\]
where
\[
C=(735,534,333,177,66,0,0,105,345),\quad
b=(40,30,20,11,4,0,0,5,16).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Add edges preserving $C_4$-freeness until no addition is possible.
The resulting $H$ has at least the original number of edges; no isolated
vertex is possible because an edge at an isolated vertex creates no cycle.
Fix an actual parity $P$. On every coordinate $Q_6$ face $F$, use the
induced parity and import $4S_F+2c_F\le632+5L_F$, with no degree
restriction on the opposite parity. Here $S_F$ is the cubic star sum,
$c_F$ the full-root count and $L_F$ the number of roots of degree at most
two. The precise historical sources and certificate scopes are recorded
in PROOF.md; the new finite enumeration does not re-prove this import.

There are112 such faces. Every specified-side cubic star occurs ten
times. A degree-$d$ root is locally full in $\binom d6$ faces and low in
\[
l_d=\sum_{j=0}^2\binom dj\binom{8-d}{6-j}
=(28,28,28,18,6,0,0,0,0)_d
\]
faces. Thus
\[
\sum_d\left(40\binom d3+2\binom d6-5l_d\right)n_d\le70784.
\]
For every $d=0,\ldots,8$ the coefficient in parentheses minus $402d$
plus1610 equals $2C_d$. Applying the two degree sums gives
$2C(P)\le276864-402m$. Nonnegativity of $C$ gives $m\le688$.

Each of the $\binom83 2^5=1792$ coordinate $Q_3$ faces has at most
one full root in the specified parity: two distinct same-parity vertices
of $Q_3$ have distance two and two common neighbors, which would give a
square if both were full. Therefore $\sum_P\binom d3\le1792$.
Subtracting $10m$ and adding $40\cdot128$ proves the stated $b$ bound.
\end{proof}

The complete unfiltered necessary integer covers at $m=683,\ldots,688$
have sizes $814,393,171,66,20,4$, respectively. Their $n_0=0$ subcovers
have sizes $783,385,170,66,20,4$. To prove completeness, enumerate the
seven coordinates $d\notin\{5,6\}$ using their positive $C_d$ costs and
solve uniquely
\[
n_6=m-\sum_{d\ne5,6}dn_d-5\left(128-\sum_{d\ne5,6}n_d\right),
\quad n_5=128-\sum_{d\ne5,6}n_d-n_6.
\]
Reject precisely negative coordinates and the $b$ violation. The saved
JSON contains every row, not only these counts. The683 unfiltered set
matches the existing814-row artifact exactly. These are relaxations,
not classifications of graphs.

\begin{proposition}
For each ambient oriented edge $uv$ and the seven external directions,
encode the three remaining square edges as states $s=a+2b+4c$. The
complete size-seven histogram inventory for missing edges in a maximal
$C_4$-free graph is necessarily $x_7\ge1$ on states $0,\ldots,7$;
for retained edges it is necessarily supported on states $0,\ldots,6$.
Both inventories have1716 rows.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A retained edge cannot have an all-three completion. A missing edge can
be added exactly when none of its seven coordinate squares has the
other three edges: every four-cycle through it is such a square.
Maximality is consequently equivalent to having a state7 completion
at every missing edge. Removing one unit from $x_7$ bijects the missing
edge inventory with size-six multisets on eight states, counted by
$\binom{13}{7}=1716$. The retained inventory consists of size-seven
multisets on seven states, also $\binom{13}{7}=1716$. Before maximality,
the missing inventory had $\binom{14}{7}=3432$ rows.
\end{proof}

All local-type weights $\lambda$, histogram weights $\beta$, parity
profiles and endpoint degrees of an actual $H$ come from that same $H$.
Their common incidence equations must therefore share the same weights
and normalization. Taking the convex union across all six edge counts
and both parity row inventories is weaker than fixing the actual $m$
and rows, but remains necessary: each actual $H$ has both parity profiles in its own fixed-$m$
component. In the parent convention $\alpha$ is half the sum of their
profile point masses, so $N_d=2\sum_p\alpha_p n_d(p)$; $\beta$
denotes edge-histogram weights. The actual $\alpha$ can have two rows
at the same $m$. Additional convex mixing across $m$ is a relaxation,
not an assertion that the actual edge count is mixed. Feasible marginal data need not be a graph. The endpoint
bridge and exact infeasibility certification are separate open work;
no LP is run here and the bound682 is not proved.

The literal682-edge seed was checked: it is $C_4$-free and maximal, with
342 missing ambient edges, having3,4,5,6 closing squares in frequencies
41,159,120,22. This checks compatibility with the known lower example,
not the nonexistence of larger examples. Exact seed hash, saved rows,
and replay command are in CHECK.json and PROOF.md.
