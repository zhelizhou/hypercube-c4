\begin{abstract}
We derive an exact finite-dimensional density bridge for the five original
full-face rooted flag spaces and the nine-state vertex-rooted square flag
space.  Face collisions and all three vertex overlap types are included
inside one actual coordinate \(Q_4\).  We then give an explicit rational
distribution on \(818\) legal \(Q_4\) graphs whose six finite-eight moment
matrices are positive semidefinite and whose mean density is
\[
 \rho_*=\frac{76799371703}{113777777664}
       >\frac{683}{1024}.
\]
It follows that no kernel choice in this fixed six-block family can certify
a local maximum below \(683/1024\).  This is a limitation of a certificate
family, not a \(Q_8\) graph or a resolution of
\(\operatorname{ex}(Q_8,C_4)\le682\), which remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{G01: actual states, common gauges and the finite score}

All graphs are finite simple spanning subgraphs of a cube, and absent
ambient edges are allowed.  A cube edge joins binary vertices at Hamming
distance one.  Every cube \(C_4\) is a coordinate square.
Let \(\mathcal K\) denote the complete set of \(C_4\)-free subgraphs of a
labelled \(Q_4\), including nonmaximal graphs.

\subsection{Five full-face state spaces}

The rooted square has vertices \(00,10,01,11\), indexed \(0,1,2,3\), and
edge order
\[
 (0,1),\quad(0,2),\quad(1,3),\quad(2,3).
\]
A root edge pattern \(r\) is a four-bit mask.  The \(D_4\) action on this
square has five orbits of nonfull root patterns, with least representatives
\[
 \mathcal R=(0,1,3,6,7).
\]
For one external direction, a full-face flag has four connecting-edge
bits, in root-vertex order, followed by the four translated-face edge bits,
in the edge order above.  Write this eight-bit code as \(f=c+16p\).
It is legal over \(r\) precisely when \(p\ne15\) and, for each square edge
\(ab\), it is not the case that the root edge \(ab\), its translated edge,
and both connectors at \(a,b\) are all retained.
This is exactly the \(C_4\)-free condition on the rooted \(Q_3\), since
its other two square faces are the root and the translated face.

The state ID is the increasing order of the legal codes \(f\), not a
separate orbit quotient of each flag.  The dimensions are
\[
 (d_0,d_1,d_3,d_6,d_7)=(240,212,190,187,170).
\]
For completeness these counts follow by summing the number of independent
connector sets for the graph \(r\cap p\).  For \(r=0\) the count is
\(15\cdot16=240\); for \(r=1\) it is \(8\cdot16+7\cdot12=212\).
For \(r=3\) it is \(4\cdot16+8\cdot12+3\cdot10=190\).
For \(r=6\) the last factor is \(9\), giving \(187\).
For the three-edge path \(r=7\), summing over all subsets of its edges,
then excluding the full translated square, gives
\[
 2(16+3\cdot12+2\cdot10+9+8)-8=170.
\]
Here the independent-set counts for one edge, a two-edge path, two
disjoint edges, and a three-edge path on four vertices are
\(12,10,9,8\), respectively.

For each canonical root choose an arbitrary real symmetric PSD matrix
\(Q_r\) in this state order.  Its root stabilizer \(H_r\) has order
\(8,2,2,4,2\), respectively.  Define
\[
 \overline Q_r=\frac1{|H_r|}\sum_{h\in H_r}P_hQ_rP_h^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{G01.1}
\]
It is PSD because it is an average of congruent PSD matrices.
An actual root square is first mapped to its canonical root by one
square isometry.  Both external flags are transported by that same map.
Any other such map differs by a stabilizer element, so
\(\overline Q_r[s,t]\) is independent of the choice.  The two flags
are never independently canonicalized.

\subsection{The original nine-state vertex flag}

At a marked vertex \(v\), an unordered direction pair \(A=\{i,j\}\)
gives the full coordinate \(Q_2\) rooted at \(v\), with \(v\) its local
\(00\) corner.  The four-bit code uses the same edge order.
Interchange of the two axes exchanges bits \(0,1\) and bits \(2,3\).
The nine ascending nonfull canonical codes are
\[
 0,1,3,4,5,6,7,12,13.
 \tag{G01.2}
\]
Indeed, the \(15\) nonfull codes have three fixed codes under axis
interchange, so their orbit count is \((15+3)/2=9\).
Write \(\eta(v,A)\) for the corresponding state and let \(Q_v\) be
any real symmetric PSD \(9\)-by-\(9\) matrix.
There is no quotient by root translation: the marked vertex is preserved.

\subsection{Exact finite-eight score}

For an actual \(K\in\mathcal K\), each of its \(24\) root squares \(F\)
has two actual external flags \(s_F,t_F\), with one common root gauge.
Set
\[
 F_8(K)=\frac1{24}\sum_{F\subset K}
 \left\{\frac56\overline Q_{r(F)}[s_F,t_F]
 +\frac1{12}\left(
   \overline Q_{r(F)}[s_F,s_F]
  +\overline Q_{r(F)}[t_F,t_F]\right)\right\}.
 \tag{G01.3}
\]
For each vertex of \(K\), its six two-direction sets have three unordered
disjoint pairs, twelve unordered pairs intersecting in one axis, and six
diagonal pairs.  Define
\[
 V_8(K)=\frac1{2688}\sum_{x\in K}
 \left\{
 30\sum_{\mathrm{3\ disjoint}}Q_v[\eta_A,\eta_B]
 +6\sum_{\mathrm{12\ overlap}}Q_v[\eta_A,\eta_B]
 +\sum_{\mathrm{6\ diagonal}}Q_v[\eta_A,\eta_A]\right\}.
 \tag{G01.4}
\]
Every entry in these sums is read from that same actual \(K\).
The family under review is exactly
\[
 \scor(K)=\frac{e(K)}{32}+F_8(K)+V_8(K),
 \tag{G01.5}
\]
with arbitrary PSD kernels in dimensions \(240,212,190,187,170,9\).
There are no additional global degree inequalities, common-\(Q_6\)
constraints, \(Q_5\) cuts, free linear corrections, or extra flag spaces
in this family.

\subsection{Exact integer normalization and implementation contract}

Suppose \(Q_b=L_bL_b^{\mathsf T}/D^2\), with integer rectangular Gram
factors.  In the source state order let
\[
 \operatorname{faceval}(r,s,t)=8D^2\overline Q_r[s,t].
\]
This is an integer by the stabilizer orders.  The denominator is
\(\mathcal D=64512D^2\), and the integer score numerator is
\begin{align*}
 Z(K)={}&2016e(K)D^2\\
 &+\sum_F\left\{280\operatorname{faceval}(r,s,t)
 +28\operatorname{faceval}(r,s,s)
 +28\operatorname{faceval}(r,t,t)\right\}\\
 &+\sum_x\left\{
 720\sum_{\mathrm{disjoint}}(L_vL_v^{\mathsf T})_{\eta_A,\eta_B}
 +144\sum_{\mathrm{overlap}}(L_vL_v^{\mathsf T})_{\eta_A,\eta_B}
 +24\sum_A(L_vL_v^{\mathsf T})_{\eta_A,\eta_A}
 \right\}.
 \tag{G01.6}
\end{align*}
Multiplication of (G01.3)--(G01.5) by \(64512D^2\) proves this formula.
A certificate crossing the desired threshold would need
\[
 \max_{K\in\mathcal K} Z(K)<43029D^2,
 \qquad 43029=64512\cdot683/1024.
 \tag{G01.7}
\]
The larger denominator requires a separately checked arithmetic bound;
the old \(192D^2\) normalization and an old asymptotic-error wrapper
cannot be substituted.

The read-only original scientific definition is in
\file{plugins/hypercube-extremal/archive/source/certificate_final_q4/verify_local.cpp}
of the MathRSI repository.  The task-local \file{finite_flags.py}
reconstructs those state orders and returns the integer upper-coordinate
coefficients of (G01.5), with denominator \(64512\).
An upper off-diagonal coordinate carries its full linear coefficient.
The original \(Q_4\) mask edge order is bottom \(Q_3\) edges, top \(Q_3\)
edges, then eight crossing edges; each \(Q_3\) uses increasing vertex
and increasing free-axis order.

\section{G02: exact finite-eight whole-domain bridge}

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-eight sampling bridge]\label{thm:bridge}
For every choice of the six PSD kernels above and every actual
\(C_4\)-free \(G\subseteq Q_8\), a uniform coordinate \(Q_4\)
restriction \(K\) satisfies
\[
 \frac{e(G)}{1024}\le \E_K\scor(K).
 \tag{G02.1}
\]
Consequently, if \(\scor(K)\le C\) for all \(K\in\mathcal K\), then
\(e(G)/1024\le C\) for all such \(G\).  In particular,
\(C<683/1024\) would imply \(e(G)\le682\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All probability spaces below are finite; rearrangement of their sums
requires no limiting or integrability assumption.

Fix one actual coordinate root square \(F\) of \(G\) and one consistent
root gauge.  It has \(q=6\) external directions.  Write \(u_F\) for their
empirical full-face state distribution and \(R_{\ne}\) for the ordered
joint distribution of two distinct external directions.  Independent
uniform draws give exactly
\[
 u_Fu_F^{\mathsf T}
 =\frac56R_{\ne}+\frac16\operatorname{diag}(u_F).
 \tag{G02.2}
\]
Taking its product with \(\overline Q_{r(F)}\) yields
\[
 \frac56\E_{k\ne\ell}\overline Q_{r(F)}[s_k,s_\ell]
 +\frac16\E_k\overline Q_{r(F)}[s_k,s_k]
 =u_F^{\mathsf T}\overline Q_{r(F)}u_F\ge0.
 \tag{G02.3}
\]
There are \(1792\) root squares in \(Q_8\), and each belongs to
\(\binom62=15\) coordinate \(Q_4\)'s.  Thus choosing a uniform \(Q_4\)
and one of its \(24\) root squares makes the global root square uniform,
and selects a uniform pair of its six external directions.
Symmetry of the matrix turns its unordered cross term into the ordered
distinct average; the equally weighted two diagonal terms give the
single-direction marginal.  Therefore (G01.3) averages exactly to
\[
 \E_KF_8(K)=
 \E_F u_F^{\mathsf T}\overline Q_{r(F)}u_F\ge0.
 \tag{G02.4}
\]

Next fix a global vertex \(x\).  It has \(28=\binom82\) unordered
direction pairs.  Let \(z_x\) be the empirical distribution of their
nine-state flags.  For independent uniform pairs \(A,B\), conditional
on \(A\), precisely \(15\) choices of \(B\) are disjoint, \(12\) share
one axis, and one is equal.  Hence
\[
 z_x^{\mathsf T}Q_vz_x
 =\frac{15}{28}R_{\mathrm{disjoint}}
 +\frac{12}{28}R_{\mathrm{overlap}}
 +\frac1{28}R_{\mathrm{same}}\ge0,
 \tag{G02.5}
\]
where each \(R\) is the corresponding conditional expected matrix entry.

These conditional distributions are exactly reproduced within a
uniform four-axis set.  A disjoint pair has a four-axis union, requiring
no completion.  An overlap pair has a three-axis union, with exactly
five choices of an unused fourth axis.  An equal pair has a two-axis
union, with exactly \(\binom62=15\) completions.  Within each category
this multiplicity is constant.  For symmetric \(Q_v\), using unordered
pairs rather than the two possible orders gives the same expected
entry.  At one local vertex the respective local averages have
denominators \(3,12,6\).  Averaging them with probabilities
\(15/28,12/28,1/28\) over \(16\) vertices gives precisely (G01.4).
Each global vertex belongs to \(\binom84=70\) coordinate \(Q_4\)'s,
so the sampled global vertex is uniform.  Thus
\[
 \E_KV_8(K)=\E_x z_x^{\mathsf T}Q_vz_x\ge0.
 \tag{G02.6}
\]
All flags in this argument are restrictions of the same original graph;
no independent face completion is used.

There are \(\binom84 2^4=1120\) coordinate \(Q_4\)'s, and each ambient
edge belongs to \(\binom73=35\) of them.  Consequently
\[
 \E_K\frac{e(K)}{32}
 =\frac{35e(G)}{1120\cdot32}
 =\frac{e(G)}{1024}.
 \tag{G02.7}
\]
Adding (G02.4), (G02.6), and (G02.7) proves (G02.1).
The asserted implications follow by averaging a uniform local upper
bound and using that \(e(G)\) is an integer.
\end{proof}

\section{G03: simultaneous transport and complete common-\(K\) coverage}

\begin{lemma}[Actual score invariance]\label{lem:symmetry}
For every fixed kernel choice, \(\scor(K)\) is invariant under
\(\Aut(Q_4)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A cube isometry permutes the \(24\) root faces.  On each actual face its
induced root-frame change is one square isometry applied to both external
flags.  Two transports to the canonical root differ by a member of its
stabilizer, and (G01.1) is invariant under that stabilizer.
The two external directions may interchange.  The cross term is
symmetric, and the two diagonal terms have equal coefficients.
Thus every transported face contribution agrees with its original one.

The isometry also permutes the \(16\) marked vertices and their four
directions.  Its induced map on a marked two-axis square preserves the
marked corner and only possibly interchanges the two axis labels.
This is precisely the quotient (G01.2).  The isometry preserves and
permutes the three disjoint pairs, the twelve overlap-one pairs and
the six equal pairs.  Their sums in (G01.4) are unchanged.
Edge density is unchanged, proving the lemma.
\end{proof}

The original common-\(K\) encoding writes \(K=(A,B,S)\), with two
same-labelled \(Q_3\) layers and crossing positions \(S\).  It has the
exact legality condition
\[
 K\text{ is }C_4\text{-free}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A,B\text{ are }C_4\text{-free and }S
 \text{ is independent in }A\cap B.
 \tag{G03.1}
\]
Indeed, a new crossing square is exactly a common layer edge whose two
endpoints both have crossing edges.
The unchanged original geometric checker reconstructs all \(2902\)
legal labelled \(Q_3\)'s, all \(48\) cube automorphisms, and the
\(90179\) pair representatives in \file{keys.bin}.  It checks the pair
orbits are disjoint and cover all \(8421604\) ordered pairs; it
enumerates all \(13150453\) legal marked completions and retains the
\(3312331\) completions having a minimum hidden-direction pair index.

Those counts and the original finite coverage code are an explicit
computational import for any full-domain native separation.  The new
score is compatible with this import because
Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetry} preserves its value under every transport used
to put a legal graph into that slice.  Merely preserving block
dimensions would not suffice.  Neither the bridge theorem nor the
family obstruction below requires a new full-domain scan: their
quantifiers range over \(\mathcal K\), and every obstruction support
graph is checked directly for legality.

\section{G05: a uniform obstruction for the fixed kernel family}

\begin{theorem}[Strict six-block family obstruction]\label{thm:obstruction}
For every choice of the six real symmetric PSD kernels in G01,
\[
 \max_{K\in\mathcal K}\scor(K)
 \ \ge\ \rho_*
 =\frac{76799371703}{113777777664}
 \ >\ \frac{683}{1024}.
 \tag{G05.1}
\]
Thus this fixed finite-eight six-block family cannot prove the desired
strict local threshold, regardless of kernel rank, numerical
optimization effort, rationalization or exact full-domain separation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every support graph of Proposition~\ref{prop:point} belongs to
\(\mathcal K\).  By the linear definition (G04.2),
\[
 \E_{p_*}\scor(K)
 =\rho_*+\sum_{b=0}^5\langle Q_b,M_b(p_*)\rangle.
 \tag{G05.2}
\]
For PSD matrices \(Q,M\), the matrix \(M^{1/2}QM^{1/2}\) is PSD, so
\(\tr(QM)=\tr(M^{1/2}QM^{1/2})\ge0\).
Every term in (G05.2) is therefore nonnegative by the proposition.
The maximum over \(\mathcal K\) is at least the expectation over this
probability distribution, proving the first inequality.
The strict comparison is the exact positive rational difference
(G04.8).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Limits of the conclusion.}
The distribution \(p_*\) is a biased distribution of legal local graphs,
not asserted to arise as the restriction distribution of a single
\(Q_8\).  PSD constrains average rooted statistics; individual local
moment matrices need not be PSD and no legal local graph was discarded
on that basis.  G05 concerns only the six kernels and exact collision
terms specified in G01.  Additional valid global constraints or new
flag spaces would constitute a different family and must be tested
against this explicit point.  G05 does not rule out the original graph
upper bound; the full \(682\) objective remains open.

\section*{Implementation provenance, not mathematical premises}

The retained Markdown contains the complete implementation narrative.
Two actual \(304\)-edge \(Q_7\) graphs supplied \(280\) common local
slices each, with density \(19/28\), but their finite-eight moments have
exact negative directions.  Their high density alone is not a family
obstruction.
A small restricted-pool rank-one LP included those slices and all
\(1120\) actual \(682\)-seed slices, yielding \(1160\) distinct local
graphs.  It ran \(35\) rounds with nonnegative ray weights and
negative-eigenvector pricing.  Its final recomputed restricted upper
candidate was approximately \(0.6759921289039941\).
The \file{working_*} files preserve every round, active graph, ray,
weight and dual probability.  That restricted number is not a
full-domain upper bound, and its numerical plateau is not a premise
of Proposition~\ref{prop:point} or Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}.
No further same-family optimization is required to verify this proof.
