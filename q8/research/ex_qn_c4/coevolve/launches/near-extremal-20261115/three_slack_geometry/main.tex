\section{S01: Original-domain caller and three units of actual slack}
Let$G\subseteq Q_8$ be any C4-free graph with683 edges, and suppose
one specified global parity has degree counts
\[
           (n_0,\ldots,n_8)=(0,0,1,4,0,70,52,1,0).       \tag{1}
\]
The opposite parity is unrestricted. The specified parity has128 vertices and degree sum683.
Use the reviewed actual-face theorem in
\texttt{../q8\_crowded\_faces/main.tex}, with exactly its declared
historical316/317/321 local imports and assurance. It gives
\[
 C(P)+\Phi_*\le1149,\qquad
 C(P)=333+4\cdot177+105=1146,\quad\Phi_*\le3.             \tag{2}
\]
For each actual coordinate$Q_6$ face$F$, let$L_F$ count specified-parity
roots of restricted degree at most two. The summand of$\Phi_*$ is
$5L_F/2-t_*(L_F)$. It is zero at$L_F=0,2$, equals$3/2$ at$L_F=1,3$,
and is at least6 whenever$L_F\ge4$. Thus every$L_F\le3$ and at most
two of the112 actual faces have odd$L_F$. In fact their number is0 or2,
since the total low-root incidence is$28+4\cdot18=100$, which is even.
Only the upper bound of two is needed below.

\section{S02: Transport from the same actual faces to direction pairs}
Denote the unique degree-two root by$z$, and the four degree-three roots
by$x_1,\ldots,x_4$, with their actual retained direction sets$A_i$,
$|A_i|=3$. Repeated$A_i$ are permitted. Every other specified root has
global degree at least five and cannot become low after two directions
are omitted. No normalization, choice of another gauge, position
classification, or condition on the retained pair at$z$ is needed.

For an omitted coordinate pair$I$, the four actual faces are
$F(I,b)$ for$b\in\{0,1\}^I$. Set
\[
 t_I=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}^I}L_{F(I,b)}\pmod2,
 \qquad T=\{I:t_I=1\}.                                 \tag{3}
\]
Each root occurs in exactly one of these four faces, with its own actual
fixed bits. The degree-two root is always low there; a degree-three root
is low exactly when$I\cap A_i\ne\varnothing$. Therefore the exact
same-object identity is
\[
 t_I=1+\sum_{i=1}^4\mathbf1_{I\cap A_i\ne\varnothing}
                                  \pmod2.               \tag{4}
\]
Every$I\in T$ has at least one odd$L_{F(I,b)}$ face above it.
Different omitted pairs have disjoint sets of actual faces. Consequently
\[
        |T|\le\#\{F:L_F\text{ odd}\}\le2.               \tag{5}
\]
This is the required bridge retaining actual fixed bits and one common
cube. It is a necessary consequence for every actual position choice,
not an assertion that a surviving direction tuple could be completed.

\section{S03: Eight odd coordinate degrees force four direction pairs}
View$T$ as a simple graph on the eight coordinate labels. Fix a
coordinate$a$. For any three-set$A$, among the seven pairs containing$a$,
the number meeting$A$ is7 if$a\in A$, and3 if$a\notin A$.
Both numbers are odd. Summing(4) over all$b\ne a$ therefore gives
\[
 \deg_T(a)\equiv7+\sum_{i=1}^4(7\text{ or }3)
              \equiv1+4\equiv1\pmod2.                  \tag{6}
\]
All eight coordinate degrees are positive odd integers. The handshake
identity yields$2|T|=\sum_a\deg_T(a)\ge8$, so
\[
                             |T|\ge4.                  \tag{7}
\]
Equations(5) and(7) contradict one another.

\begin{theorem}
The complete one-sided degree profile(1) cannot occur in any683-edge
C4-free subgraph of$Q_8$, with the opposite parity unrestricted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An arbitrary graph in this domain satisfies S01. Its five actual low
roots, retained direction masks and all112 fixed-bit faces give S02,
regardless of their common positions and permitting repeated masks.
The elementary parity argument S03 contradicts S02. Hence no such graph
exists.
\end{proof}
