\title{A common-face parity obstruction for two degree-three and two degree-four roots}
\maketitle
\section{T01: Exact original-domain caller}
Let$G\subseteq Q_8$ be any C4-free graph with683 edges and one
specified parity profile
\[
           (n_0,\ldots,n_8)=(0,0,0,2,2,80,40,3,1).       \tag{1}
\]
The opposite parity is unrestricted. The specified class has128 roots
and degree sum683. The reviewed actual-face theorem in
\texttt{../q8\_crowded\_faces/main.tex}, with its declared historical
316/317/321 imports and assurance, gives
\[
 C=2\cdot177+2\cdot66+3\cdot105+345=1146,
 \qquad\Phi_*\le3.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Every actual coordinate$Q_6$ face has a specified-side low-root count
$L_F$. The loss$5L_F/2-t_*(L_F)$ is zero for$L_F=0,2$,
$3/2$ for$L_F=1,3$, and at least6 for$L_F\ge4$. Thus all$L_F\le3$
and at most two actual faces have odd$L_F$. Their total number is even
because the total low incidence is$2\cdot18+2\cdot6=48$.
Only the upper bound of two will be used.

Let$x_1,x_2$ be the actual degree-three roots with retained masks$A,B$,
and$y_1,y_2$ the actual degree-four roots with masks$R,S$.
All four positions are distinct and have the same parity; the retained
masks may repeat. Other specified roots have degree at least five,
so none becomes low on a$Q_6$ face. All faces and masks below refer to
one common cube, not separately normalized observations.

\section{T02: Actual low-face sets and an intersection bound}
For any actual root$v$, let$W(v,M,d)$ be its set of containing$Q_6$
faces on which a retained mask$M$ of size$d$ gives degree at most two.
Faces are labeled by an omitted pair$I$ and their actual two fixed bits.
At$d=3$, this set consists of the face with bits$v_I$ for each pair
meeting$M$, so it has size18. At$d=4$, the pair must be contained in$M$,
so the set has size6. Put
\[
 U=W(x_1,A,3),\quad V=W(x_2,B,3),\quad
 P=W(y_1,R,4),\quad Q=W(y_2,S,4).
\]
The actual odd-face set is exactly
\[
                       O=U\mathbin\triangle V
                           \mathbin\triangle P\mathbin\triangle Q,
               \qquad |O|\le2.                         \tag{3}
\]
This is a literal identity of112-bit face sets, retaining fixed bits.
No compatibility or completion of independently selected local faces
is being assumed.

\begin{lemma}
If$A\ne B$, then$|U\cap V|\le10$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let$D$ be the set of coordinates where$x_1,x_2$ differ. Distinct
same-parity positions have$|D|\ge2$. A common containing face must
omit a pair in the agreeing coordinates$C=[8]\setminus D$, whose
size is at most six, and that pair must meet both$A$ and$B$.
Enlarge$C$ to any six-set$H$; this only adds possible pairs.
Put$A'=A\cap H$, $B'=B\cap H$. If either has size at most two,
at most$\binom62-\binom42=9$ pairs meet it. Otherwise both have
size three, and since$A\ne B$ they are distinct, with
$s=|A'\cap B'|\le2$. Inclusion--exclusion counts the pairs meeting
both as
\[
 \binom62-\binom32-\binom32+\binom s2
                         =9+\binom s2\le10.
\]
A pair determines at most one common actual fixed-bit face, which
proves the assertion.
\end{proof}
If$A\ne B$, this lemma and$|U|=|V|=18$ give
$|U\triangle V|\ge16$. Since$|P\triangle Q|\le12$, the elementary
set inequality$|X\triangle Y|\ge|X|-|Y|$ gives
\[
                         |O|\ge16-12=4,                \tag{4}
\]
contrary to(3). Thus any hypothetical input must have$A=B$.

\section{T03: Equal three-masks force equal four-masks}
For each omitted pair$I$, sum the four actual fixed-bit low counts
modulo two. If$A=B$, the two degree-three contributions cancel,
regardless of their different positions. The directional parity is
\[
       t_I=\mathbf1_{I\subseteq R}+\mathbf1_{I\subseteq S}
                                  \pmod2.               \tag{5}
\]
Every$I$ with$t_I=1$ has at least one actual odd face above it;
different$I$ have disjoint face fibers. Hence
$\#\{I:t_I=1\}\le|O|\le2$.
If$R\ne S$, let$r=|R\cap S|\le3$. Their six-pair incidence sets
have symmetric difference of size
\[
                         12-2\binom r2\ge6,             \tag{6}
\]
contradicting this bound. Therefore$R=S$ as well. This argument permits
all repeated-mask inputs from the outset; it does not assume a
representative direction table is complete.

\section{T04: The equal-mask case still forces four actual odd faces}
Now assume$A=B$ and$R=S$. Apply one common translation to the entire
marked cube, sending$x_1$ to0 and$x_2$ to the nonzero even position
with support$D$. All other positions and every fixed-bit face are
transported by this same translation.

If$|D|\ge4$, there are at most four agreeing coordinates and at most
six common containing$Q_6$ faces. Thus$|U\cap V|\le6$,
$|U\triangle V|\ge24$, and$|O|\ge24-12=12>2$.
It remains that$|D|=2$. Write$h=|D\cap A|$.
Common low faces correspond exactly to pairs among the six agreeing
coordinates meeting$A\setminus D$, so
\[
       |U\cap V|=\binom62-\binom{3+h}2.                \tag{7}
\]
For$h=1,2$, these intersections are9,5 respectively. Hence
$|U\triangle V|$ is18 or26, and again subtracting the at-most12
faces in$P\triangle Q$ leaves at least6 odd faces.

Finally suppose$D\cap A=\varnothing$. The exact symmetric difference
$U\triangle V$ is supported on the six omitted pairs
\[
                         I=\{a,d\},\quad a\in A,d\in D. \tag{8}
\]
For each such pair there are exactly two distinct actual faces in the
symmetric difference: the face with fixed bits0 and the face with
fixed bits equal to$D_I$, which has one bit equal to one. For all other
pairs the two degree-three low-face contributions cancel or are absent.
The sets$P,Q$ have no face above any pair not contained in$R$.
Because$|R|=4$ whereas$|A\cup D|=5$, at least two of the six pairs
in(8) are not contained in$R$: if some$a\in A$ is missing, the two
pairs with that$a$ are missing; if all of$A$ is contained, some$d\in D$
is missing, giving three missing pairs. Above each such missing pair,
the two actual faces from$U\triangle V$ cannot be canceled by$P$ or$Q$.
Therefore
\[
                              |O|\ge4,                  \tag{9}
\]
contrary to(3). All cases have now been exhausted.

\begin{theorem}
No683-edge C4-free subgraph of$Q_8$ has profile(1) on either parity,
with the opposite parity unrestricted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An arbitrary such graph gives the four actual roots and odd-face bound
in T01--T02. If$A\ne B$, (4) contradicts that bound. If$A=B$, T03
forces$R=S$, and T04 gives a contradiction in every possible distance
and retained-mask intersection. No arbitrary common position was
removed and no separate gauges were combined.
\end{proof}
